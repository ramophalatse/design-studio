Money Learnings: Financial Freedom Programme

Fit Finder Portfolio Review

Seven concepts, five life moments, one council. Each idea was pressure-tested individually by a six-lens Fit Finder council (Contrarian, Expansionist, Logician, Researcher, Buyer, Fit Finder) against its own prototype and team feedback. This is the read across all seven together, the patterns no single concept review surfaces on its own, and a sequencing recommendation for what to validate first.

7 concepts reviewed 4 of 5 life moments addressed by design 1 kill · 6 reshape (1 split) Prepared 20 July 2026
Executive summary

No concept is ready to build as prototyped. All but one are worth continuing.

Across all seven concepts, the same shape kept recurring: a real, well-evidenced problem; a genuinely strong strategic instinct (every Expansionist review scored 8 or higher); and an execution detail (usually a safety, privacy, or governance gap) that the team's own internal review had already sensed but not resolved. Only one concept, Embedded Messages, has a contradiction at its core that no amount of redesign fixes. Everything else earns a reshape, not a rebuild from zero.

Reshape: 5 concepts Split: 1 concept (keep half, cut half) Kill: 1 concept
The most consistent finding isn't about any one concept; it's about the portfolio's working method. Every "Evidence" section across all seven briefs cites internal team reaction. None cite a survivor or a support-organisation reviewer. That gap is cheap to close and should close before any concept goes further, not after.

Cross-cutting patterns

Four things true of the portfolio, not any single concept

These recur across three or more concepts independently; they're portfolio-level fixes, not per-concept notes.

01

Privacy promises consistently outrun the technical build behind them

"No trace," "confidential," "stored on-device," "not shared with anyone" appear in four separate concepts, and in every case, the council found a real gap between the promise and what the architecture actually does. Quiet Confirmation's exit swaps the visible screen but leaves the real URL in browser history. Check-in & Reflect's on-device badge sits two screens above a feature that requires server-side transaction analysis. Big Greenbacks pays a reward for "engagement" it has promised never to observe. Financial Agency Review's confidentiality assumes a device the abuser doesn't also have legitimate access to.

Evidence: flagged independently by the Logician on Concepts 03, 04, and 07, and by the Contrarian on Concept 06: four different reviews, same structural fault.
02

Every concept is validated by the team that built it, not by anyone it's for

Read the "Evidence" line on all seven briefs back to back: "the team responded strongly," "the team viewed this positively," "widely viewed as elegant." Not one cites a conversation with a survivor or a support organisation. For most product ideas that's a normal, fixable gap. For this portfolio it's a materially higher-stakes gap, because the population in question cannot be casually usability-tested the way a typical feature can.

Evidence: the phrase "no user testing was discussed" or equivalent appears in the source brief for six of the seven concepts.
03

Where Nedbank tries to be the specialist, precedent says don't; where it funds and refers, precedent says it works

Every bank actually operating in this space at scale (CommBank, Monzo, NatWest, Lloyds, Barclays) converged on the same shape: a small, continuously-trained specialist team or a fund-and-refer partnership with an established NGO, never a generalist frontline employee or an in-house support group. Nedbank's own existing POWA relationship is the underused asset across this entire portfolio; it's mentioned as a helpline link in three concepts and treated as a genuine delivery partner in none of them.

Evidence: Researcher findings on Concepts 02, 04, 06, and 07 all independently cite the same set of bank precedents.
04

The strategic instinct is consistently stronger than the execution: the Expansionist scored 8+ on every single concept

That's not a coincidence worth ignoring: it means the underlying bets (borrowed trust, private retail moments, an anonymous web entry point, reflection over diagnosis, a narrow banking role, a give-back economy) are sound. What drags each concept down is never the idea; it's a specific, nameable execution risk. That's a good place for a portfolio to be: it means the fix is sharper scoping and safety work, not new ideas.

Evidence: Expansionist scores: 8, 8, 9, 8, 8, 8, 8 across Concepts 01–07 respectively.

Recommended sequencing

What to validate first, in order

Every concept needs work before it needs code. This orders that work by how cheap it is to de-risk and how much it unblocks downstream.

Phase 0Before touching any prototype further
The single next action

Get a specialist reviewer in the room. One conversation with POWA (or a Surviving Economic Abuse–style specialist) reviewing Concepts 03, 04, 05, 06 and 07 against a single question each (does the safety mechanism hold, is a bank the right messenger) resolves more risk than any further design iteration.

  • Audit the four "no trace" claims (Concepts 03, 04, 06, 07) against what the actual tech stack does: analytics, backups, joint-account visibility, shared-device access.
Phase 1Cheapest to ship safely
  • Borrowed Reach, reshaped to a take-home artifact, single-partner pilot.
  • Big Greenbacks, give-back slice only: donation and Greenbacks-matching routed to POWA, no in-house group facilitation.
Phase 2No partner dependency, good discovery asset
  • Quiet Confirmation: fix the exit mechanism and remove the decoy note before anything else touches it. This is the only concept discoverable by non-customers on the open web.
Phase 3Consolidate the in-app layer
  • Check-in & Reflect, reshaped off the home screen, with the account-pattern comparison cut or re-consented, absorbing the video content from Video-led Reflection as an embedded module rather than shipping either as a separate destination.
Phase 4Highest organisational investment
  • Financial Agency Review, rebuilt around a small, trained specialist team (not branch-wide staff) with a scripted handoff to named external partners.

Concept 01 · Problem-Unaware

Borrowed Reach

Reshape

Beauty-retail brand partnerships (Swiitch Beauty and similar) as a discreet entry point.

The call: kill the in-store QR scan; ship a take-home artifact instead: a card slipped into the bag at checkout, opened alone later. Reconcile with Nedbank’s existing “How Free Are You?” campaign before any further spend.

Six-persona council

Independent reviews from a Contrarian (red team), an Expansionist (bull case), a Logician (first-principles), a Researcher (web evidence), a Buyer (in-character target customer), and a Fit Finder (Problem-Solution / Product-Market / Business Model diagnostic).

Contrarian3/10

Partner brands’ commercial incentive is inverted: a bring-down message doesn’t fit an indulgent retail space, and discretion and reach are structurally opposed: safe means small, visible means unsafe. There’s no mechanism from reflection to action, a QR handoff at a beauty counter is a bad disclosure point, and the reputational asymmetry is stark: the partner brand gets the upside, Nedbank owns the downside.

Expansionist8/10

This buys trust infrastructure that already exists rather than building it from scratch. Private retail moments (mirror, bathroom) are rare unmonitored windows. A 10x version licenses a reflection-prompt module across the whole partner roster, generating an anonymised “Freedom Index” dataset, the cheapest top-of-funnel play in the entire portfolio.

Logician3/10

A physical QR insert directly contradicts the stated discretion premise. The trust-transfer assumption is unproven (likely paid placement, not organic) and there’s no falsifiable unit of success. The targeting logic (these brand audiences are enriched for the target condition) is asserted, not derived.

Researcher6/10

Nedbank already runs a live “How Free Are You?” campaign: real risk of duplication that needs reconciling. The beauty-brand partnership channel is validated in South Africa (Avon × 1000 Women Trust), and the market is real and large (HSRC: roughly 1 in 8 women; 30–43% economic-abuse prevalence in relationships). Global precedent (CommBank, HSBC) shows awareness spikes cheaply but rarely converts to specialist help.

Buyer4/10

Beauty-retail is for escape, not confrontation; a heavy question fights the mood, and public QR scanning is the opposite of discreet. Nedbank’s name triggers a “sales pitch” filter, and “take control of your financial independence” reads as patronising. A take-home format would beat an in-store scan.

Fit Finder3.5/10

Breaks at Product-Market Fit: no partner or user has actually said yes to anything yet. Missing evidence: one real pilot readout with an actual partner brand. (PS 4 · PM 3 · BM 6)

Concept 02 · Problem-Unaware

Embedded Messages

Kill

Hidden prompts and QR codes in packaging, tissue paper, and clothing tags.

The call: kill. The contradiction is structural, not a rough edge. Only revive with a discovery mechanism that requires no phone, no scan, no trace.
Problem-Solution
3
Product-Market

5

Six-persona council

Independent reviews from a Contrarian (red team), an Expansionist (bull case), a Logician (first-principles), a Researcher (web evidence), a Buyer (in-character target customer), and a Fit Finder (Problem-Solution / Product-Market / Business Model diagnostic).

Contrarian2/10

The discovery mechanism doesn’t exist: any signal loud enough for the target is loud enough for an abuser standing next to her, and it ignores digital surveillance entirely (a QR scan leaves a browser-history trail). “Low complexity” hides real operational complexity (partner vetting, crisis protocol), and there’s zero evidence base; the team liked it for simplicity, not efficacy.

Expansionist8/10

This is an infrastructure layer, not one idea; it scales via other people’s supply chains (pharmacy bags, salons, delivery boxes). The discovery paradox dissolves via ubiquity: a generic motif everywhere becomes camouflage, and the QR interaction itself is the real signal. Zero-CAC awareness at population scale, if it worked.

Logician2/10

Discovery versus discretion is a logical impossibility, not a design constraint: you can’t titrate a signal to be noticeable-by-target and invisible-to-adversary when both have equal sensory access to the same object. A QR nobody knows to scan is inert print, a chicken-and-egg problem: you need pre-existing awareness for the awareness mechanism to work at all.

Researcher3/10

The UK’s “Ask for ANI” pharmacy codeword scheme (government-backed, 5,000+ locations) is the closest real precedent, and it reached only around 186 people in nearly three years before being cancelled. “Ask for Angela” shows the same awareness-versus-execution gap even with huge PR (93% public awareness, only 36% of venues actually running it). QR codes are structurally the wrong mechanism for “hidden”: deliberately unlabeled codes underperform baseline 1–5% scan rates. The one precedent that consistently works (the salon “Cut It Out” programme) succeeds because a trained human initiates conversation, not a passive hidden object.

Buyer3/10

Wouldn’t recognise brand-y language (“free yourself”) as meant for her. The QR scan itself is the risk, not the safety feature; it’s a footprint on a device that might be checked. Packaging and tags get thrown away within about 5 minutes, and that’s the entire discovery window.

Fit Finder3/10

Breaks at Problem-Solution Fit. Missing evidence: a moderated read-test with real women in a realistic unboxing or fitting-room context; currently zero data points exist anywhere. (PS 3 · PM 2 · BM 5)

Independent reviews from a Contrarian (red team), an Expansionist (bull case), a Logician (first-principles), a Researcher (web evidence), a Buyer (in-character target customer), and a Fit Finder (Problem-Solution / Product-Market / Business Model diagnostic).

Contrarian4/10

The quick-exit widget is itself a liability confession: it documents that Nedbank anticipated coercive surveillance of its own domain. “Doesn’t track” is unverifiable against a standard corporate site’s analytics/CDN stack. No feedback loop exists (privacy by design is unmeasurable), so the organisation can’t tell if it’s working or just reassuring people into inaction.

Expansionist9/10

The highest score in the portfolio. This is the only concept discoverable by non-customers on the open web: SEO-able, linkable by NGOs without branding Nedbank as “the abuse bank.” The four reflection questions already map to the validated SEA-12 risk tiers and could become an anonymous, aggregate self-triage instrument feeding the rest of the roadmap. Adjacent: white-label the “disguised editorial page” pattern to other stigmatised financial moments, such as debt shame, divorce, or elder abuse.

Logician5/10

The URL is the actual trace: a content-swap decoy doesn’t touch browser history or DNS logs; a real fix needs history-rewrite, not just a visible-screen swap. The leftover “this is a decoy” note visible on the exit page is a live safety defect, not a cosmetic leftover, and must be removed before anything ships. The four questions and tone are the strongest part of the whole concept; no fix needed there.

Researcher7/10

Quick-exit buttons are mature, sector-standard practice (NNEDV, Women’s Aid; a peer-reviewed CHI 2023 study of 727 implementations), but the described mechanism (instant content-swap) is the weaker of two known methods per that same research; URL-overwrite is the safer pattern. Every DV org’s own guidance explicitly warns this won’t stop someone with device access from finding out, and the page’s current copy overclaims relative to sector norms. Direct precedent exists (CommBank Next Chapter), and Nedbank already has a POWA partnership to build on.

Buyer6/10

The headline (“Maybe it’s nothing. Maybe it isn’t.”) genuinely rings true. Would NOT type into the free-text field regardless of promises; her threat model is the shared device or iCloud, not the website. The POWA number is the single most useful thing on the page and it’s buried near the bottom. Her real access pattern is fragmented, stolen minutes; the page assumes one continuous visit and offers no safe way to resume without restarting.

Fit Finder4/10

Breaks at Business Model Fit: a page correctly designed to leave no trace has, by that same design, no way to prove its own value to whoever has to keep funding it. (PS 6 · PM 4 · BM 3)

Six-persona council

Independent reviews from a Contrarian (red team), an Expansionist (bull case), a Logician (first-principles), a Researcher (web evidence), a Buyer (in-character target customer), and a Fit Finder (Problem-Solution / Product-Market / Business Model diagnostic).

Contrarian3/10

The threat model is inverted: the population most at risk is also most likely to be surveilled, via a phone or app checked by a controlling partner. On-device storage protects data from Nedbank, not from an abuser with legitimate account or device access. Peer benchmarking, the team’s favourite mechanic, may backfire via minimisation (“only 10%, so I’m probably fine”) or hand an abuser ammunition. Nedbank-gating filters out exactly the higher-risk, less-banked population.

Expansionist8/10

The peer-benchmarking layer is the real product: a proprietary behavioural-control dataset nobody else has, potentially an annual “State of Financial Control” report. The team’s own suggested de-scope, dropping the transactional dependency, is the actual unlock: it makes the concept bank-agnostic and licensable industry-wide, and sidesteps the denial/minimisation failure mode of clinical abuse-screening tools.

Logician5/10

“Stored on device, not shared with anyone” doesn’t survive account-access reality. The two comparison layers are not actually on-device by construction, directly contradicting the privacy badges shown two screens earlier. The most-loved mechanism, benchmarking, is the least private part, and “lean into benchmarking” plus “simplify” pull in opposite directions.

Researcher6/10

CommBank Next Chapter is direct, strong precedent that this works at scale: its ML scanning blocked 458,000-plus abusive transaction messages in a year. The five questions map to a real validated instrument, the Scale of Economic Abuse (SEA-12); adopt that language instead of inventing new wording. Transactional inference needs real legal care: POPIA treats this as approaching “special personal information” and requires prior authorisation for profiling. Device or app visibility to an abuser is a documented IPV risk the brief doesn’t address at all.

Buyer5/10

The reflection or summary mechanic, playing her own words back to her, is genuinely powerful. The account-pattern comparison is the trust-breaker: it reveals the bank was already capable of connecting “abuse signal” to real transaction data, flipping the whole feature from reflection tool to covert monitoring in her mind.

Fit Finder4/10

Breaks at Business Model Fit: the team’s own suggested improvements (go cross-bank, lean into benchmarking) directly undercut Nedbank’s specific ROI case, and nobody has named a success metric. (PS 6 · PM 5 · BM 4)

The highest Buyer score in the portfolio. Would install it in a heartbeat, “and that’s exactly what should worry you.” The quick-exit worked perfectly in the one real moment she needed it and sold her on the whole feature. Would take the meeting but “watching the exits the whole time.” Wants strictly narrow scope (accounts, visibility, ownership, next step); the moment it drifts emotional or therapeutic from an unqualified person, trust breaks. Confidentiality versus verification is a logical impossibility: paying Greenbacks for “showed up” or “engaged” requires someone to report attendance back to a rewards ledger, which IS a disclosure. Untrained peer facilitation is a safeguarding hole and an access vector. The reward currency is denominated in the exact thing weaponised against these women, inverting therapeutic logic into a gameable, farmable metric.

Researcher3/10

Peer support itself is validated (Growth Circle research shows real reductions in stress and cortisol), and the five-session/buddy core is the one piece with real evidence. But gamifying trauma disclosure has documented ethical backlash in the clinical literature. Banks in this exact space consistently choose refer-and-fund over build-and-run (NatWest funds SafeLives, Barclays funds Refuge, Nedbank itself already partners with POWA rather than delivering directly); no precedent of a bank directly running trauma support groups. Donation-via-loyalty-points is proven and easy (Amex, Chase, Marriott, Red Cross), the one piece with direct precedent.

Buyer6/10

As a survivor: the “You Belong Here” framing genuinely lands, but earning points for showing up to talk about her own trauma is a coin-flip between validating and cheapening; would NOT become a Buddy on a fixed five-session timeline. As an ordinary customer: would donate around R200 on impulse but would NOT volunteer as a facilitator without knowing who vets the survivors and the volunteers.

Fit Finder3/10

Breaks at Product-Market Fit: five bundled propositions (rewards, support groups, mentorship, donations, volunteering), no single clear value proposition. (PS 5 · PM 3 · BM 2)

Methodology.
