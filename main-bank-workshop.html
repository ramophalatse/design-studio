South African retail banking / Executive ideation / 6 October 2026

Worth choosing.
Again and again.

Earn a place in customers’ everyday lives. Turn distinctive value into a useful first experience, a lasting routine and a sustainable main-bank relationship.

The overarching challenge

How might we make the value of banking with us so relevant, visible and easy to experience that customers want to join—and keep choosing us?

01 / Frame the ambition

A relationship, not an account count.

These customer contexts overlap. A graduate can be an employee, support a family and run a side business. Start with a trigger and a job, then use the lens to expose different needs. No bank capabilities, budgets, partners or delivery commitments are assumed.

What “main bank” should mean

A working definition to agree in the room: the bank reliably handles a customer’s chosen core money jobs, receives relevant regular income where applicable, and remains useful across successive cycles. Corroborate the customer’s stated main bank with consented behavioural signals: recurring inflows, essential payments and sustained everyday use. A salary received then immediately swept elsewhere is not sufficient proof.

For irregular income, use relevant earning or household cycles. Agree eligibility, observation window and measurement rules before experiments; do not equate income, wealth or app opens with relationship value.

FOMO grounded in value

Something useful that people do not want to miss: progress, relevance, belonging, access, shared clarity or less effort. Ask what improves in the customer’s life when they participate, and what remains valuable after the first encounter.

Commercial test: will useful repeat behaviour cover acquisition, service, partner and risk costs? Keep credit uptake, unnecessary spending, artificial urgency and dependence out of the success story.

Two separate creative tracks

A · Activate largely fixed propositions. Work with verified existing products and benefits; explore communication, discovery, distribution, partnerships, support and engagement. A partnership that changes entitlements or the core service belongs in B.

B · Shape future propositions. Explore benefits, bundles, journeys and partnership models. Any pricing, business-model or regulated-service change requires commercial, risk and regulatory assessment before a delivery commitment.

Evidence key: Documented fact = what a source says the offer does. Company/funder-reported result = an attributed outcome, with limits. Interpretation = the mechanism we infer. Proposed banking application = a hypothesis to test. Public documentation confirms an offer exists; it is not independent verification of every customer outcome.

02 / Distinct design challenges

Choose the behaviour to earn.

Four primary briefs and one cross-cutting brief for safe migration. Each contains a customer tension, commercial reason, separated opportunities, measures, guardrails and an executive decision.

Creative track · applies to briefs and cases
01 / YouthFirst value → everyday use

Make independence tangible

How might we help people establishing financial independence turn a first banking experience into a useful everyday routine?

Primary behaviour

Repeat the customer’s chosen everyday money task through this account after completing it once.

Explore the brief

Customer tension

Public context: Stats SA reports Q1 2026 unemployment of 60.9% among economically active 15–24-year-olds and 40.6% among 25–34-year-olds [S1]. These are age-band labour-market rates, not a description of every young customer. Research hypothesis: the bank’s promise may feel distant when study, irregular earnings or a first job create immediate tasks and uncertainty.

Commercial reason

Repeat use can turn low-value account openings into an active relationship and, as income develops, a credible destination for it. Neither employment nor future profitability is guaranteed.

Track A · Activate fixed propositions
  • Show the existing proposition through a relevant task, such as receiving a bursary or paying for transport, using verified benefits only.
  • Test discovery through education or community channels, subject to access and permission; provide supported onboarding and a first-task demonstration.
  • Follow up at the next relevant life event with practical help and optional peer stories, without public balance comparisons.
Track B · Shape future propositions
  • Explore an independence journey combining banking with credible learning, earning or administrative support; test partner appetite before promising access.
  • Explore flexible bundles for changing income and life stages, including a transition from study to work. Pricing and eligibility need commercial, risk and regulatory assessment.

Mechanisms that could fit

Progress and access: SA Youth makes useful opportunities discoverable with low access friction; GoTyme shows contextual, supported entry; Monzo makes incoming money useful. Relevance is the reason to participate, rather than an age-based badge.

Primary behavioural measure

Repeat-task rate: eligible onboarded participants who complete their selected task and repeat it in the agreed observation window ÷ all eligible onboarded participants. Define the task and window before the pilot.

Customer guardrail
Task completion effort, accessible help and cost to use; include irregular-income and low-data users.
Economic guardrail
Acquisition and support cost per repeat user; incremental contribution after service and partner costs.
Trust guardrail
Comprehension of fees and eligibility; no implied job guarantee, pressure to spend or unnecessary sharing of young people’s data.
Executive decision / assumption

Is the barrier a lack of practical first value, or a proposition that does not fit? Test a task-led Track A journey against current onboarding before funding a broader Track B bundle.

Starter experiment · proposed

Prototype two first-task journeys with students, graduates and first-income earners; then test the stronger supported journey in a consented cohort. Compare actual repeat-task use with usual onboarding, alongside effort and support cost.

Relevant cases: E1 SA Youth / Harambee, E5 TymeBank → GoTyme: meet people in context, E6 Monzo: move, then organise payday

02 / FamiliesEveryday use → deepening → staying

Coordinate without losing control

How might we make coordinating household money easier while protecting each person’s independence?

Primary behaviour

Use the bank consistently to meet an agreed recurring household obligation.

Explore the brief

Customer tension

Research hypothesis: coordinating contributions, bills and dependants can create invisible work, uncertain responsibilities and privacy concerns. Validate with couples, multigenerational households, co-parents and people supporting relatives across homes; do not assume one address, shared ownership or equal income. Starling and Spotify verify different coordination mechanisms, not this South African customer insight.

Commercial reason

A recurring household job can create predictable inflows and useful payment activity. More linked accounts do not by themselves demonstrate greater household value or profit.

Track A · Activate fixed propositions
  • Explain existing account and payment options around one household job, with clear ownership and fee implications.
  • Offer a supported household planning conversation and a contribution calendar; use only payment and permission features already available.
  • Make existing benefits understandable for each eligible person and provide reminders chosen by the customer.
Track B · Shape future propositions
  • Explore permission-based coordination, contribution visibility and separate personal control, rather than assuming a joint account.
  • Explore a household service bundle with clear individual entitlements, optional shared administration and a safe separation journey. Bundling, pricing and liability need commercial, risk and regulatory assessment.

Mechanisms that could fit

Shared clarity and individual agency: Starling separates purposes and provides joint spending tools; Spotify puts independent experiences under one bill. Borrow the coordination principle, not their household definition or ownership rules.

Primary behavioural measure

Recurring-obligation rate: pilot households meeting their agreed obligation through the bank in each observed cycle ÷ eligible pilot households. Agree what counts as an obligation and the cycle window before testing.

Customer guardrail
Coordination effort and perceived fairness; late or missed essential payments.
Economic guardrail
Net household contribution after support, bundle and partner costs; distinguish new value from fee cannibalisation.
Trust guardrail
Individual consent, visibility boundaries and safe revocation; exclusion and financial-control concerns; no involuntary sharing or dependence on a controlling payer.
Executive decision / assumption

Would shared administration improve value more than shared pricing? First test whether people understand and want the proposed permissions and can exit safely.

Starter experiment · proposed

Use a role-based prototype around a real recurring bill; observe contribution planning and revocation, including unequal incomes and separate homes. If acceptable, run a concierge pilot comparing obligation completion and effort with the existing journey.

Relevant cases: E2 Starling: joint money and Spaces, E7 Spotify Platinum: together, individually, E6 Monzo: move, then organise payday

03 / Side-hustlersFirst value → repeated inflows → deepening

Turn earning into usable money

How might we help people managing employment and additional income handle each earning cycle with less effort and more clarity?

Primary behaviour

Route repeated side-income settlements into the account for a self-chosen money-management job.

Explore the brief

Customer tension

Research hypothesis: collecting payment, reconciling receipts and separating money for personal needs or business costs may become fragmented alongside a salaried job. Test different earning patterns and business maturity. Yoco verifies a payment-and-reporting offer [S6–S8]; it does not establish how common these tensions are among salaried side-hustlers.

Commercial reason

Repeated inflows and useful payment services can deepen activity and fee or balance value. A business settlement account may remain secondary for personal banking; measure both relationships separately.

Track A · Activate fixed propositions
  • Demonstrate existing receiving, payment-reference and record-keeping options around one actual earning cycle.
  • Test distribution through relevant merchant communities or service providers where access is agreed; provide clear onboarding and settlement expectations.
  • Help customers use available statements and account distinctions to reconcile income. Do not imply that a personal account permits business use without checking terms.
Track B · Shape future propositions
  • Explore a service journey joining payment acceptance, reconciliation, expense separation and cashflow visibility.
  • Explore optional partner services and a bundle that evolves with usage. Pricing, acquiring, settlement liability and personal/business eligibility require commercial, risk and regulatory assessment.

Mechanisms that could fit

A completed earning job: Yoco joins payment collection to a record and payout; Starling offers allocation of incoming money. Repeat usefulness comes from getting work done, not driving more spending or borrowing.

Primary behavioural measure

Repeat-settlement rate: eligible pilot earners receiving settlements in successive agreed earning cycles ÷ eligible pilot earners. Define eligible receipts and exclude self-transfers from the measure.

Customer guardrail
Time to usable funds, reconciliation effort, dispute handling and total service cost.
Economic guardrail
Net contribution per active earner after payment processing, fraud, support and partner costs; avoid counting salary and merchant income twice.
Trust guardrail
Clear settlement terms and personal/business boundaries; consent for business data; no pressure to take funding or misleading tax advice.
Executive decision / assumption

Is the priority getting paid, organising receipts or allocating income? Identify the most costly failure before selecting a partner or building tools.

Starter experiment · proposed

Observe one full earning cycle across several side-income models. Test a concierge journey for the leading task against usual practice, measuring repeated receipts, time to usable funds and cost to serve.

Relevant cases: E3 Yoco: payment to usable receipt, E2 Starling: joint money and Spaces

04 / EmployeesSalary move → first value → ongoing routine

Make payday work harder

How might we make choosing a bank through the workplace lead to a payday routine employees find worth keeping?

Primary behaviour

Receive successive salary payments into the account while continuing a chosen payday routine.

Explore the brief

Customer tension

Research hypothesis: access through an employer may make joining convenient without making a salary move worthwhile; employees may also fear employer visibility or feel pressured. UK payroll-savings trials show that journey design can affect saving participation [S9], but do not establish South African needs or prove main-bank conversion.

Commercial reason

Sustained salary inflows can fund everyday use and stable balances, and workplace access may lower distribution cost. Saving through payroll alone does not mean the salary account has moved.

Track A · Activate fixed propositions
  • Test voluntary workplace discovery and private bank support, subject to employer agreement; let people decline without consequence.
  • Explain payroll cut-offs and assist the employee with existing salary-update procedures, checking receipt after payday.
  • Demonstrate existing allocation and payment options at payday; suggest optional routines only where supported today.
Track B · Shape future propositions
  • Explore a permission-based payroll-linked savings or allocation journey with easy pause, withdrawal and portability when employment ends.
  • Test employer-funded support or a benefits model, with explicit boundaries around data and employee choice. Default settings, payroll deductions and pricing require commercial, risk and regulatory assessment.

Mechanisms that could fit

Distribution at a meaningful trigger: Nest Insight’s trials reduce saving friction at payday; Monzo connects incoming salary to allocation. Adapt timely usefulness and clear control, not automatic account selection or UK default amounts.

Primary behavioural measure

Sustained-payday rate: consenting salary movers who receive successive salaries and continue their selected routine over the agreed window ÷ all consenting movers in the pilot.

Customer guardrail
On-time salary receipt, understanding, affordability and easy pause; late essential payments.
Economic guardrail
Distribution, payroll integration and support cost per sustained user; incremental balances and contribution, net of employer subsidy.
Trust guardrail
Private employee choice; no employer access to individual banking data; no savings deduction without a locally approved consent model; safe exit after job changes.
Executive decision / assumption

Does workplace access plus immediate payday value outperform workplace sign-up alone? Separately test whether any employer will fund or support the model.

Starter experiment · proposed

With a willing employer, compare voluntary task-led onboarding with usual sign-up support in equivalent cohorts. Track salary receipt and continued routine use. Test payroll-linked saving separately; do not infer salary migration from savings uptake.

Relevant cases: E4 Nest Insight: payroll savings trials, E6 Monzo: move, then organise payday

05 / Across contextsJoining → income move → continuity

Make the move feel safe

How might we help customers move the banking jobs they depend on without losing continuity or control?

Primary behaviour

Complete a customer-chosen migration of regular income and essential payments, with continuity verified.

Explore the brief

Customer tension

Research hypothesis: perceived effort and fear of salary or bill disruption can stop an interested customer from moving important jobs. Monzo explicitly distinguishes account opening from switching and identifies payments and permissions that require separate action [S13]. This proves journey complexity, not the prevalence of the barrier in South Africa.

Commercial reason

A safe completed move can convert an acquired account into a relationship used for core jobs. A sign-up, salary-update request or balance transfer is only a leading indicator.

Track A · Activate fixed propositions
  • Use a clear checklist, human support and existing procedures to help customers choose what moves and when.
  • Check payroll deadlines, payment recipients and recurring card payments; confirm actual first receipts and bills, with customer permission.
  • Offer phased migration and visible status rather than assuming closure of the old account or automatic redirection.
Track B · Shape future propositions
  • Explore a migration service with permissioned status tracking and partner coordination, backed by clear exception handling.
  • Consider service commitments only after delivery and liability are validated. New guarantees, integrations or subsidies need commercial, risk and regulatory assessment.

Mechanisms that could fit

Orchestration and reassurance: Monzo’s UK switch service makes responsibilities explicit; GoTyme’s supported access shows that completion can sit in everyday settings. Neither the UK switching infrastructure nor a retail footprint is assumed available here.

Primary behavioural measure

Verified-migration rate: consenting movers whose chosen income and essential-payment jobs work through the bank over the agreed cycle window ÷ all consenting movers starting the journey.

Customer guardrail
No avoidable interruption to income or essential payments; customer effort and recovery time.
Economic guardrail
Cost per verified move, remediation and ongoing contribution; include failed moves and account duplication costs.
Trust guardrail
Explicit scope and permissions; honest status, easy cancellation and reliable fraud checks; old-account closure stays under customer control.
Executive decision / assumption

Is migration assistance the missing step between interest and main-bank use? Test a manual service before investing in automation or promising a guarantee.

Starter experiment · proposed

Pilot a human-supported checklist against current migration support. Check actual income and essential-payment continuity for all starters, including failures; agree recovery steps and stop conditions before launch.

Relevant cases: E6 Monzo: move, then organise payday, E5 TymeBank → GoTyme: meet people in context

Measures are proposed definitions, not targets. Establish a baseline, denominator, agreed window and smallest worthwhile change before any live test. Read economic and trust guardrails alongside the primary behaviour.

E1 / South Africa · non-profit opportunity networkUseful progress

SA Youth / Harambee

Make a next step towards earning accessible before asking for a deeper relationship.

Active offer; read 6 Oct 2026

Youth

Experience, business logic and evidence

What it is · documented offer

Harambee manages SA Youth, a free learning-and-earning opportunity network for people aged 18–34 with a South African or refugee ID [S2].

How a customer experiences it

Register, browse local opportunities and work-readiness resources, apply and update the profile. The mobi site is zero-rated on listed participating networks; toll-free guides support users [S2]. External application sites may have different access requirements.

Mechanism · interpretation

Interpretation: relevant opportunities reduce the distance between joining and a useful next step. New opportunities create a legitimate reason to return; access itself is valuable even before employment follows.

Who provides value, who pays, how it reaches people

Harambee and opportunity providers supply the network; government and philanthropic support fund the public-benefit model [S3]. Young people do not pay to join. Work-seeker channels and guides connect supply and demand. Ongoing funding and credible opportunity supply sustain it; partner-by-partner commercial terms are not established here.

What the evidence does and does not prove

Verified offer: registration, resources and zero-rating [S2]. Funder-reported scale: Co-Impact’s January 2025 brief reports more than 1.4 million work opportunities connected since launch [S3]. Opportunities are not unique people or necessarily new permanent jobs. This is not independent causal proof, banking conversion evidence or a current October 2026 count.

Transferability · hypothetical application

Youth. Adapt low-friction discovery and a useful next step tied to a customer’s transition. A bank is not an employment network; jobs, zero-rating and partner access cannot be assumed or made conditional on banking.

Track A · Activate fixed propositions

Which verified existing benefit could help a young person complete a meaningful task immediately, through channels they already use?

Track B · Shape future propositions

What credible learning or earning support could complement banking, and who would supply and fund it?

Risks and prerequisites

Opportunity quality, support capacity, identity and data handling, exclusion of ineligible users and costs beyond the zero-rated site. Keep consent specific and promises realistic; preserve access for people who decline the banking offer.

Sources: S2 Harambee: Work-seekers / SA Youth, S3 Co-Impact: Harambee, January 2025 brief (PDF)

E2 / United Kingdom · retail bankShared clarity

Starling: joint money and Spaces

Make money’s purpose visible, and give shared obligations a clear place to live.

Active offer; read 6 Oct 2026

Families, Side-hustlers

Experience, business logic and evidence

What it is · documented offer

Starling offers joint accounts, bills management and goal Spaces. Both joint applicants need eligible personal accounts [S4].

How a customer experiences it

Customers can separate money by purpose, manage shared expenses and track joint goals [S4–S5]. Split Payment distributes incoming money across Spaces and eligible accounts in one flow [S5]. Spaces separate money within the app [S20]. Do not assume every personal-account feature also works in joint accounts.

Mechanism · interpretation

Interpretation: visible purposes and shared administration can reduce uncertainty about what money is for. Bills and changing goals create repeated usefulness and an opportunity to deepen a relationship.

Who provides value, who pays, how it reaches people

Starling supplies the bank and app; customers access it digitally. Its FY2025 accounts identify net interest income as the main revenue source, alongside fees and commissions [S16]. Free-to-use features can support a broader deposit and payment relationship; their specific incremental economics are not disclosed here.

What the evidence does and does not prove

Verified offer: product journeys [S4–S5]. Commercial evidence: dated group financial accounts [S16]. No feature-level evidence reviewed establishes that Spaces or joint tools cause main-bank adoption, better household outcomes or lower churn.

Transferability · hypothetical application

Families; secondarily side-income allocation. Adapt purpose visibility and explicit responsibilities. A two-person joint account does not map to every household, and UK eligibility, tax examples and deposit rules do not transfer.

Track A · Activate fixed propositions

How could existing payment and account options make one household obligation easier to coordinate today?

Track B · Shape future propositions

What permissions would let people coordinate a shared job while keeping personal money private?

Risks and prerequisites

Ownership, mandate and liability clarity; abusive control, separation and bereavement; insufficient funds in the correct place; accessibility and service costs. Test revocation and exception handling. Extra accounts should not be the measure of success.

Sources: S4 Starling: Joint account, S5 Starling: Split Payment, S16 Starling Bank: FY2025 accounts (PDF), S20 Starling: Spaces

E3 / South Africa · merchant payment servicesComplete the earning job

Yoco: payment to usable receipt

Join collection, confirmation and records around a task people already need to complete.

Active offer; pricing has evolved

Side-hustlers

Experience, business logic and evidence

What it is · documented offer

Yoco offers payment links, in-person acceptance and merchant management tools. Current pricing includes a free Core tier and paid service tiers [S6–S8].

How a customer experiences it

An eligible merchant creates a link, shares it through chat, email or an invoice, and the buyer pays on a secure page. The merchant receives a notification and sales record [S6]. The online offer supports consolidated sales and payouts [S7]. Payment confirmation and money being available are distinct events.

Mechanism · interpretation

Interpretation: the value is getting paid with a usable record, without building an online shop. Each earning cycle makes the service useful again; tools can grow with the business.

Who provides value, who pays, how it reaches people

Yoco supplies acceptance and tools. Merchants pay transaction fees and, where chosen, hardware or tier fees [S8]. Distribution includes direct online sign-up and shareable links [S6–S7]. Commercial interpretation: sustainable economics depend on processing margin, volume, fraud and support costs; no margin is assumed.

What the evidence does and does not prove

Verified offer and pricing structure [S6–S8]. No controlled result reviewed establishes that payment links improve side-hustler profitability or move their main personal bank. Company growth and promotional claims are not used as causal evidence.

Transferability · hypothetical application

Side-hustlers. Adapt the complete earning job and stage-appropriate onboarding. Yoco is not proof that salary and side-income should share one account; acquiring capabilities, integrations and business-use permissions require validation.

Track A · Activate fixed propositions

Where could the bank make its existing receiving or reconciliation experience clearer around a real earning cycle?

Track B · Shape future propositions

Which payment-to-record gap is valuable enough for a customer or partner to pay to solve?

Risks and prerequisites

Fraud, chargebacks, settlement delays, cash users, affordability and data privacy. Define customer support and liability across partners. Avoid treating funding uptake or increased spending as the success metric.

Sources: S6 Yoco: Payment Links, S7 Yoco: Online Payments, S8 Yoco: Plans and pricing

E4 / United Kingdom · employer-distributed financial serviceA repeatable payday routine

Nest Insight: payroll savings trials

Change the effort needed to establish a useful habit at a natural recurring moment.

Historical trials; final report March 2025

Employees

Experience, business logic and evidence

What it is · documented offer

UK payroll-saving trials at SUEZ/TransaveUK and Bupa Care Services and Co-op/Wagestream launched in 2021–2022; final report March 2025 [S9].

How a customer experiences it

Opt-out journeys set an adjustable, stoppable £40 monthly contribution into accessible savings [S9]. This historical specification should not be presented as a current offer.

Mechanism · interpretation

Interpretation: workplace reach plus recurring payroll reduces setup and follow-through effort. Customer control and ready access are central; silence is not evidence that someone understands or values a default.

Who provides value, who pays, how it reaches people

Employers distribute and integrate payroll; providers operate savings; employees contribute their money. Research had philanthropic/public-benefit support; trial savings were fee-free [S9]. Commercial contract prices and scalable margins are not established.

What the evidence does and does not prove

At four months, pooled benefits-app saving participation was 68% under opt-out versus 15% under opt-in. App-user trials were randomised; SUEZ was pre/post [S9, printed pp. 28, 33]. This does not establish South African salary switching or main-bank adoption.

Transferability · hypothetical application

Employees. Adapt payday timing, low effort and reversible control. Do not import a UK default, amount or consent model. Saving can remain with a provider while salary still reaches another bank.

Track A · Activate fixed propositions

How can existing payday support make a voluntary saving or bill-payment routine easier to establish?

Track B · Shape future propositions

What employer/provider model could make a useful routine easy, private and portable without coercion?

Risks and prerequisites

Affordability, payroll errors, employer visibility, withdrawals, job changes and local consent requirements. Test employee comprehension and outcomes before defaults. A savings pilot should not promote borrowing or fee-based wage access.

Sources: S9 Nest Insight: Easier to save, March 2025 (PDF)

E5 / South Africa · retail bankSupported entry

TymeBank → GoTyme: meet people in context

Bring account completion and physical access into places people already visit.

Materially changed in 2026

Youth, Across contexts

Experience, business logic and evidence

What it is · documented offer

TymeBank’s historical retail-kiosk model supported digital account opening and card issuance [S10]. The bank announced its transition to GoTyme on 4 February 2026 [S11].

How a customer experiences it

Historically, customers could complete identity verification and receive a personalised debit card at retail kiosks; the bank claimed opening in under five minutes [S10]. Today’s model includes TFG and Boxer kiosks and a planned mall Customer Hub rollout [S12]. Specific local availability must be checked.

Mechanism · interpretation

Interpretation: contextual distribution plus supported completion reduces the gap between interest and usable access. A card and opened account are enabling steps; meaningful first value requires a funded customer task.

Who provides value, who pays, how it reaches people

The bank supplies accounts and onboarding; retailers provide access points. Commercial interpretation: transaction services and a deposit relationship must fund bank operations plus physical delivery. Retail partner fees, revenue sharing and per-site profitability are not disclosed in the sources reviewed.

What the evidence does and does not prove

Verified historical model and bank-stated completion claim [S10]. Current change: the bank announced Pick n Pay kiosk removal and the end of double Smart Shopper points from 31 March 2026, while cash-in/out continued [S12]. No reviewed evidence attributes main-bank use or profitable retention to kiosks.

Transferability · hypothetical application

Youth and safe migration. Adapt supported completion in relevant everyday contexts. Do not assume kiosk hardware, retail partners, identity checks or five-minute delivery are available to this bank.

Track A · Activate fixed propositions

Where could an agreed distribution partner help a customer complete an existing journey and their first useful task?

Track B · Shape future propositions

Which mix of digital support and physical access creates enough customer value to cover delivery costs?

Risks and prerequisites

Partner dependence, support quality, public-space privacy, exclusion and kiosk uptime. A changing footprint needs continuity communication. Rewards are not the mechanism selected here; no instant-onboarding commitment is proposed.

Sources: S10 TymeBank: Making of a digitally smart bank (historical), S11 GoTyme: TymeBank becomes GoTyme, 4 Feb 2026, S12 GoTyme: Retail presence changes, 2 Mar 2026

E6 / United Kingdom · retail bankOrchestrate the move

Monzo: move, then organise payday

Give the move an explicit process, then make the incoming money useful immediately.

Current UK journeys; Salary Sorter introduced 2019

Across contexts, Employees, Youth, Families

Experience, business logic and evidence

What it is · documented offer

Monzo offers a UK Current Account Switch Service (CASS) journey and Salary Sorter. Sorter launched in September 2019; current payday pages describe reuse of preferred allocations [S13–S15].

How a customer experiences it

Choose a switch date: eligible balances and payment arrangements move and the old account closes. Recurring card payments and third-party permissions need separate action [S13]. Salary Sorter separates incoming money into spending, bills and savings; saved allocations can be reused [S14].

Mechanism · interpretation

Interpretation: migration orchestration tackles the cost of moving; useful allocation gives salary a reason to stay. These are different jobs, rather than one generic acquisition campaign.

Who provides value, who pays, how it reaches people

Monzo supplies the bank and app; CASS supplies shared switching infrastructure. The switch is free [S13]; banking and optional subscriptions support the wider model. FY2025 company reporting says 67% of customers joined through word of mouth [S17]. That is a dated channel claim, not a feature effect.

What the evidence does and does not prove

Verified current journeys [S13–S14]; historical launch record [S15]. No feature-level causal evidence reviewed shows that CASS or Sorter creates main-bank use or retention. Whole-bank financial and referral results do not establish that link.

Transferability · hypothetical application

Safe migration and employees; also income routines for youth and families. Adapt clear responsibilities, exceptions and useful allocation. UK CASS guarantees, timelines, Bacs early payment and old-account closure do not transfer automatically to South Africa.

Track A · Activate fixed propositions

Which existing migration steps could be clarified and supported until actual income and essential payments work?

Track B · Shape future propositions

What orchestration and allocation service would materially reduce migration risk and make payday worth returning for?

Risks and prerequisites

Wrong payroll dates, failed bills, outdated card subscriptions, fraud and false reassurance. Any service commitment needs validated recovery capacity and liability. Keep closure optional; avoid assuming all incoming money is disposable.

Sources: S13 Monzo: Switch to Monzo, S14 Monzo: Payday and Salary Sorter, S15 Monzo: Introducing Salary Sorter, 26 Sep 2019, S17 Monzo: Annual report 2025 overview

E7 / South Africa · subscription entertainmentOne promise, individual experiences

Spotify Platinum: together, individually

Make collective value easy to administer while preserving a personal experience for each member.

Current Platinum offer; legacy Family restricted

Families

Experience, business logic and evidence

What it is · documented offer

Spotify’s South African Premium Platinum offer includes up to three accounts for people at the same address [S18]. Local Family support says that plan is only available to users who signed up before 13 November 2025 [S19]; do not present the old six-person offer as open to new customers.

How a customer experiences it

One person pays a single monthly bill and invites two more household members. Each has separate credentials, playlists and listening; other members cannot see their listening. Platinum bundles enhanced features; included audiobook hours are for the plan manager only [S18].

Mechanism · interpretation

Interpretation: shared administration and personal relevance make a combined offer understandable. Its appeal can include enhanced experience and reduced coordination effort as well as price; it is not proof that bundling is always desirable.

Who provides value, who pays, how it reaches people

Spotify supplies platform access. The plan purchaser pays a recurring subscription; invitations distribute access within the household [S18]. Commercial interpretation: renewal revenue must cover content and service costs. Plan-specific margins and retention effects are not established.

What the evidence does and does not prove

Verified local offer, member boundaries and legacy restriction [S18–S19]. No performance evidence reviewed demonstrates Platinum’s household retention or incremental lifetime value. Broader subscriber growth would not isolate this mechanism.

Transferability · hypothetical application

Families. Adapt one clear promise, explicit entitlements and personal agency. Same-address eligibility and entertainment economics do not map to multi-home financial support, bank mandates or account ownership.

Track A · Activate fixed propositions

Could existing individual entitlements be explained as a coordinated household experience without changing products or pricing?

Track B · Shape future propositions

Which shared service is worth bundling, and how would each person keep access and control if the payer leaves?

Risks and prerequisites

Payer dependence, unequal entitlements, subscription affordability, household exclusion and service loss on cancellation. Test comprehension, opt-out and standalone value. A future bank bundle needs commercial, risk and regulatory assessment.

Four stories, four different jobs.

Suggested live sequence: SA Youth → Starling → Yoco → Nest Insight. Start with a non-bank example of practical progress, move to shared money, then earning and workplace routines. Assign an owner and decision date. Agree a baseline, comparison, observation window, success threshold and stop conditions before launch. Leave with test decisions, not delivery promises.

How to move from inspiration to an experiment
  1. Validate the tension. Observe real tasks and recent behaviour across overlapping contexts, income patterns, access needs and household structures. Do not use an executive preference as customer evidence.
  2. Test the promise and first value. Use a prototype or concierge journey. Observe completion, understanding and effort; preference or sign-up intent alone does not establish sustained behaviour.
  3. Run a bounded behavioural pilot. Compare with the current journey, ideally by random assignment where practical. Otherwise state selection effects and confounding. Keep assistance consistent and include dropouts and failures in the denominator.
  4. Check continued value and economics. Follow several relevant income or usage cycles, as agreed in advance. Measure the primary behaviour plus guardrails and fully loaded costs. Ask whether activity is incremental or shifted from another bank product.
  5. Decide. Continue, revise or stop using criteria agreed before results. Do not substitute registrations, clicks, sentiment or lending uptake for the chosen behavioural outcome.
06 / Concept → assumptions → experiment

Make it testable.

Build one concept at a time. Save several to compare their rating profiles. This file works locally; notes are saved in this browser when storage is available. Export a session JSON for a transferable backup. Sources open online.

Prioritise with evidence, not a beauty contest.

Use the same scale for each dimension: 1 = weak or fails; 2 = plausible with material gaps; 3 = credible with evidence and manageable gaps; 4 = strong relevant evidence. Unknown is unvalidated, not a low score. Ratings are judgement, not research results or financial forecasts.

Desirability

Does it solve a validated job, including underserved users?

Behavioural impact

Will it change the chosen recurring behaviour, beyond sign-up?

Commercial viability

Will incremental value cover full costs without harmful incentives?

Feasibility

Are capabilities, operations and partner appetite credible?

Trust

Is choice informed, reversible, private, inclusive and safe?

Do not average away a trust failure. Any dimension at 1 needs redesign; any unknown needs a targeted test. A candidate can be explored when all five are credible, but launch still depends on delivery and commercial, risk and regulatory assessment. Use the weakest dimension and next test to compare concepts; the profile is more useful than a total.
