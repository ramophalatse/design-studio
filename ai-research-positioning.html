01 · Position

There are things AI can help us do, that we simply couldn't do before. And there are things it tends to do that we should never do.

The question isn't whether or not to use AI in research. It's how to use it in ways that accelerate and broaden access to high quality insights.

The breakthrough

Scale and depth, together

  • Quantitatively significant samples (n=50+) engaged at a level of qualitative depth no human-run study could manage at that scale
  • Research can practically shift from a one-off event to always-on learning cycles
Don't quant the qual

A theme mentioned once can matter more than one mentioned a hundred times.

Pillar 01

AI-moderated interviews

What is it?

LLM-run conversations with real respondents.

How AI enables it

Runs hundreds of sessions concurrently with dynamic probing.

Practical use cases

Concept validation, churn diagnostics, brand tracking.

Pillar 02

Synthetic personas

What is it?

Digital twins trained on behavioral data.

How AI enables it

Simulates response patterns instantly and at near-zero marginal cost per run.

Practical use cases

Rapid-testing messaging and edge cases before committing resources to validation.

Pillar 03

AI-assisted synthesis

What is it?

A spectrum from automated transcription to thematic clustering.

How AI enables it

Surfaces candidate themes across transcripts far faster than manual grouping/theming.

Practical use cases

Turning raw transcripts into thematic decks, each theme traced to a verbatim quote or timestamp.

Nielsen Norman Group benchmarked a synthetic-user platform against three of their own studies. Synthetic responses tracked broad attitudinal direction but were one-dimensional next to real interviews. Their recommendation: use synthetic users to prepare for research, not replace it.

Rosala & Moran, "Synthetic Users: If, When, and How to Use AI-Generated Research," Nielsen Norman Group, June 2024 nngroup.com/articles/synthetic-users

Appendix · GrowthOS (Accenture Song)
What it actually is

A consulting engagement with a platform attached: a dedicated squad (Project Lead, Growth Strategy Lead, Value Architect, Researcher/Insights), a Leadership Team for oversight, and paid "Expert Hours" for prompt design, insight interpretation, and validation strategy. The deliverable is executive-ready artifacts, not raw output.

Where it sits

It spans the full lifecycle: synthesis, synthetic sentiment (Proxies), evidence-led ideation, validation experiments, concept sharpening, and opportunity prioritization with TAM/TAP sizing. That last stretch sits entirely outside the three pillars above; neither Maze nor Outset compete there.

Access model

Both deployment options, Accenture-hosted SSO or a custom-built client environment, are enterprise-wide commitments. Neither is a team-level pilot the way Maze or Outset are.

Bank-specific delivery risk

Accenture's own sample timeline for a "banking client with complex SSO" shows five months to the first use-case session, with authentication setup flagged Medium, not High, delivery confidence.

Cost model

Pricing is tiered by active users and bundles fixed expert hours, structurally different from Maze's and Outset's usage-based licensing.
