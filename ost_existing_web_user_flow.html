01
Entry hook: Speak peer-to-peer, lead with the trades

Unlike the new customer flow, this speaks to someone who already trusts the platform — "you already invest with us" acknowledges their existing relationship rather than selling from scratch.

The pip visualisation makes the 5 trades feel concrete and countable, not abstract. The value range ("R125 – R500+") reflects real brokerage variability rather than a fixed promise.

Design intent: The pip row immediately below the offer banner visually anchors the 5 trades as a tangible asset, something the user can picture spending rather than a vague discount.
02
Why switch: Utility first, reward second

Existing web users have established habits. This screen earns the behaviour change by leading with app-specific utility (speed, alerts, watchlist) before re-anchoring the offer.

The unlock condition is tied to a first in-app trade, not just a login. This ensures the incentive drives the actual behaviour change rather than a passive migration that doesn't stick.

Open question: Should the unlock condition be first login (lower friction, more migrations) or first trade (higher friction, but ensures real engagement)? Recommend first trade, it's the habit we're actually trying to build.
03
First trade: The waiver is visible at the point of execution

The brokerage fee waiver is called out directly on the order confirmation screen — "this is free trade 1 of 5" so the reward is felt in the moment of highest emotional engagement, not as a post-trade receipt.

The CTA "Confirm trade — free!" makes the zero-cost nature of the action explicit at the moment of commitment, reducing hesitation.

Design intent: Naming the trade number ("1 of 5") at execution creates immediate awareness of the remaining credits, priming the user to think about their next 4 trades before they've even placed the first.
04
Trade tracker: 4 remaining credits drive return visits

The pip tracker persisting post-trade is the highest-retention element in this flow. Four remaining free trades are a standing reason to return to the app, each one is a pull mechanic, not a push.

The 90-day expiry adds mild urgency without pressure. At day 75, a proactive reminder ("15 days left on your free trades") could drive a spike in trading activity before expiry.

Design intent: The "App trader" badge anchors a new identity for this user. They're no longer a web user who occasionally checks in, they're an active app trader. The price alert nudge points to the next action naturally.
Open question: What is the confirmed validity window for the 5 trades? The 90-day assumption used here needs business confirmation before this goes into build.
