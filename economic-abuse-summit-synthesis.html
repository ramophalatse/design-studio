Three things to carry into the meeting

These are the findings that most change or sharpen the working recommendation in the brief.

The front line is the product

Banks that report results (BNZ, HSBC, Lloyds, Commonwealth Bank) built a small trained team, a simple spot-and-refer habit for everyone else, and a handover where she never repeats her story. This supports assisted banking as the core.

Brief: Section 04, Section 05, Commitment 04

Authority decides what is possible

The recurring blocker is rules that need the abuser's signature or presence. Every working scheme answers one question first: what can a bank act on without him, and who may approve an exception?

Brief: Section 06, Commitment 03 (authority column)

Three gaps stay open elsewhere too

Money to leave, debt treatment, and funded partner capacity are weak almost everywhere. The brief already treats them as conditions. The summit suggests naming a single owner for each before any public promise.

Brief: Section 07, Section 08, Commitments 02 and 05

Practical takeaways

Eighteen actions drawn from the themes. Use now needs only existing staff, process or governance and an owner. Test first depends on a product, policy or legal fact that Nedbank owners must confirm. Later needs a build, funding or law, so it stays out of the November promise.

What this evidence cannot tell you

Practice is self-reportedBank numbers come from the bank's own speaker. They show what was built, not independent proof that outcomes improved.
Other markets, other lawSafe-harbour rules, credit-bureau law and privacy exemptions differ by country. South African rules were not covered, so each borrowed action needs a Nedbank legal check.
Mostly very large banksWestpac, Lloyds, HSBC and Commonwealth Bank run dedicated teams. Nepal, PNG and BNZ show smaller starting points, and BNZ is the closest model for a lean launch.
Prevalence is not comparableMeasures vary widely, from 11.3% to 100% among survivors in the global review. Do not mix these figures with the HSRC rates already in the brief, or use them to size demand.
