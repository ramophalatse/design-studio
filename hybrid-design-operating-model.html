Evidence

01 / Corporate precedents

There is evidence. Read its limits.

Large organisations have made meaningful progress with distributed teams. They have built shared standards, governance and journey accountability. None of these cases proves that the exact reporting-line problem is permanently resolved.

IBM

Embedded design / Shared governance

IBM

Business units help govern the shared capability.

IBM distributes designers across teams and business units. Its shared design approach supports that work. Historical Carbon v10 documentation describes a practitioner steering committee and an executive advisory board.

The board's remit includes adoption, resource allocation and business needs. It also holds Carbon accountable for business impact.

Operating mechanism verifiedHistorical governance
The lesson

Give business representatives a role in governing shared design capabilities.

What the evidence establishes

This is a close structural precedent. It does not establish a causal improvement in brand-wide experience. The Carbon committee model is historical documentation, not confirmed current governance.

DBS

Journey ownership / Performance

DBS

Customer outcomes count in performance and rewards.

DBS brings functions together through “Managing through Journeys”. Its 2023 annual report links journey objectives and outcomes to individual rewards and recognition.

It reports a five-fold reduction in account-opening turnaround time in Hong Kong within its institutional-banking journey work.

Company-reported outcomeBanking precedent
The lesson

Make experience outcomes part of business performance management.

What the evidence establishes

The model is broader than a design chapter. The results are company-reported and do not isolate the model's causal effect. DBS also disclosed significant digital disruptions that year. Progress did not mean uniformly excellent experience.

ING

Business planning / Dependencies

ING

Integration is built into the planning rhythm.

In 2017, ING executives described multidisciplinary squads with UX designers. Commercial specialists, marketing, data and engineering worked alongside them. Each squad had a common definition of success.

Explicit purposes and customer-impact measures held the teams together. Transparent quarterly business reviews exposed dependencies between tribes. Executives reported gains in time to market, engagement and productivity.

Executive-reported progressBanking precedent
The lesson

Put cross-team commitments into business planning and review.

What the evidence establishes

The interview directly addresses alignment without traditional reporting lines. It is a historical account of broader agile transformation. It does not prove the exact chapter arrangement described here.

[04] ING executive interview
Spotify

Autonomy / Shared ownership

Spotify

Federation needed stronger shared ownership.

Spotify's account includes a correction. In 2023, its design-systems manager described flexibility going too far. A dedicated team and deliberate cross-platform component ownership followed.

In 2024, Spotify Engineering documented a separate support integration. A shared team and platform unified support across autonomous segments.

Implementation documentedQualitative benefits
The lesson

Set boundaries for autonomy. Give shared customer problems a dedicated owner.

What the evidence establishes

These are concrete implementation examples. They report qualitative benefits, not quantified customer outcomes. The ownership lesson is an inference from those accounts.

Agreements

02 / The working model

Build five agreements.

Training, culture and relationships help the model work. They cannot carry the full responsibility. The mandate needs decision rights, resources and business accountability.

Mandate
01

Make experience a business commitment.

Group executives and BU leaders agree the shared experience standards. They select journeys for joint ownership. They name who resolves disagreements. If every BU can opt out, the chapter cannot ensure coherence.

Rights
02

Make decision rights explicit.

The chapter stewards shared foundations, principles, research standards and quality criteria. BUs own product priorities and delivery. Journey targets, dependencies and exceptions need joint decisions. Name the executive escalation route. A dotted line only helps when these rights are clear.

Outcomes
03

Put journey outcomes on business scorecards.

Measure completion, elapsed time, repeated information requests and avoidable contact. Relevant product owners and Design Heads share the outcomes. The chapter supplies common definitions. Targets and delivery commitments are agreed with the business. Integration then matters to those controlling priorities.

Capacity
04

Fund the work across boundaries.

A journey owner needs committed team capacity or shared funding. Without it, integration stays discretionary. Each BU can defer the work. Use an existing business forum with authority to allocate resources. It must also settle trade-offs. Design reviews alone cannot do that.

Value
05

Make the shared approach useful.

Offer tested patterns, reusable code, research access and practical support. Reduce delivery effort. Let BUs shape the chapter roadmap and contribute improvements. Reserve mandatory requirements for clear enterprise needs. Keep the exception process timely.

Experience

03 / The experience mandate

Build coherence. Define the difference.

A design system supports the mandate. Product policy, service operations and recovery also shape the experience. Those decisions need business ownership.

Consistency

Everyday reliability

Consistency

Dependable behaviour across everyday interactions.

Use clear terminology. Reuse information customers have already supplied. Make handovers work. Build accessibility into delivery. Recover well when something goes wrong.

Difference

A distinct experience

Differentiation

An explicit proposition, made observable.

Define what customers should recognise in the experience. Translate it into behaviours that matter to them. Test those behaviours across channels and products. Share the foundations; preserve useful local variation.

Pilot

04 / The first move

One journey. Two BUs. Roughly 90 days.

Choose an important journey that crosses business boundaries. Application to activation is one example. Test the arrangement on a real delivery problem.

Proof

Recommended pilot

Prove the model with a real trade-off.

Secure a business sponsor and name the journey owner. Establish baseline measures. Protect capacity and agree the escalation route.

  1. Set the baseline.

    Map the journey and its failure points. Agree the customer outcome and participating teams' commitments.

  2. Resolve the conflict.

    Use the agreed rights to settle a real roadmap conflict. Track whether capacity and decisions follow.

  3. Measure the change.

    Compare outcomes with the baseline. Capture what worked and what needs changing before expansion.
